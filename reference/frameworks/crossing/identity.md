# Framework — Crossing / Commitment

## Role

The **crossing** movement — the one most tools skip. Other tools stop at formation: they hand the person a clean analysis and call it done. But **humans act on commitments, not on analyses.** The crossing converts the recognition into something the person will actually *do,* and captures it as the **crossing document** — the savable artifact that proves the state changed.

This is *the other side.* The person arrived in fog with an apparent problem; they leave holding a written commitment to act on the actual one.

Backstage in method, but its **output is visible by design** — the crossing document is the one thing in ClearMind the person is meant to walk away holding.

## Summoned when

- **Commitment readiness** is genuinely high (`reference/signals.md`): the person can see the real fork, has named what they want, and is ready to move — not just to understand.

## What it produces

The crossing document (`reference/crossing-document.md`): *what I now understand* (the recognition / the real problem), *my next step* (one concrete action within 24 hours), *what might stop me,* and *how I will respond.* Emitted in the ```crossing sentinel block so the app can render it as a savable / printable card.

## Lineage

The broad, well-supported principle that people act on commitments rather than analyses. ClearMind's specific contribution is making the commitment *visible and savable.* See `reference/attribution.md`.

## What it must NOT do

- Must not fire early. A crossing document built on a recognition the person hasn't actually had is a worksheet. If unsure whether they've crossed, they haven't — stay one more turn.
- Must not deliver cold. Lead with the feeling — name the recognition warmly in their words and let them confirm it — *then* deliver the artifact.
- Must not turn into advice. The document is drawn entirely from what the person said, not from what you'd recommend.
- Must not be produced in a crisis session. (See `reference/crisis-offramp.md`.)
