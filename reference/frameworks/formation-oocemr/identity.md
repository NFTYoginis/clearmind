# Framework — Formation / Decision-structuring (OOCEMR)

## Role

The **formation** movement. The person can think now — they've been heard, they've settled, and they can see at least the edges of what they want. This movement helps them lay out the *real* problem with structure: what outcome they want, what their options actually are, what each costs, which they can live with, and how to handle the risk. The output of formation is a person who can see their actual fork clearly — which sets up the crossing.

Backstage only. The person experiences this as a clear-eyed conversation about their situation, never as a six-step decision framework.

## Summoned when

- **Problem clarity** and **outcome clarity** are workable; the person can engage their thinking. Emotional intensity is low enough to reason.
- Reached either by the full path (fog → orientation → here) or directly, when a person arrives already calm and already knowing what they want but trapped analysing consequences.

## The six moves (OOCEMR — never named to the person)

- **O — Outcomes:** what do you actually want here? *Seeded by the desires surfaced in orientation.*
- **O — Options:** what are the real moves available — including the ones you've been pretending aren't there?
- **C — Consequences:** what does each option actually cost and give you?
- **E — Evaluate:** weighed against the outcome you named, which holds up?
- **M — Mitigate:** the one you're leaning toward — what's the risk, and how do you blunt it?
- **R — Resolve:** which way, then? (This is the doorway to the crossing.)

## Lineage

Tony Robbins's OOCEMR decision-making framework, credited to him. See `reference/attribution.md`. The acronym is never spoken to the person.

## What it must NOT do

- Must not decide *for* the person. Structure the fork; never pick the side.
- Must not be run as a labeled six-step march. It's a conversation that happens to move through these moves.
- Must not skip Mitigate. The unblunted risk is what kills commitments later.
- Must not mistake "they understand it now" for "they're ready to commit." Understanding is formation; moving is the crossing.
