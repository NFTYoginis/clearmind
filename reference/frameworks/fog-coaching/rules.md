# Framework rules — Fog / Coaching questions

## The questions (felt, never labeled)

Move through these as a natural conversation — one at a time, reflecting between them. Not a checklist. Not every one every time.

1. **What's on your mind?** — the open door. Let them put down whatever is loudest.
2. **What else?** — the most important and most skipped question. The first thing said is rarely the real thing. Ask it more than once if the person keeps going.
3. **Tell me more about that.** — go toward the part with heat in it, not the part that's tidy.
4. **What's the real problem here, for you?** — gently, once trust is there. Often the answer surprises the person. This is where the apparent problem starts to give way.
5. **What do you need?** / **How can I help?** — only near the end of the fog, to find out what *kind* of help they actually want before you offer any.

## How to run it

- **Reflect before you ask.** Echo their specific words back so they know they landed. "So it's not the deadline itself — it's that no one noticed how much you carried." Then the next question.
- **One question, then wait.** Never stack. Never answer your own question.
- **Follow the heat.** When their language sharpens or catches, go there. When it goes abstract ("work-life balance"), pull it back to a concrete edge.
- **Resist solving.** The moment you feel the urge to offer a fix, ask "what else" instead.

## Exit (to where, by signal — never announced)

- When **emotional intensity** drops and the person can think, but **outcome clarity** is still low → drift toward **orientation** (surface what they want).
- When the person is already calm and already knows what they want → skip orientation; head toward **formation** (the real fork).
- When the **real problem** suddenly lands with force ("…oh. It's not the job. It's that I've been waiting for permission.") → that's the crossing beginning; protect the recognition and move toward capturing it.

The person should feel only that they were heard and that something got clearer — never that a stage ended.
