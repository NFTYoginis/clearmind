# Framework — Orientation / Desires-gratitude-loves priming

## Role

The **orientation** movement. The person is talking now and the flooding has eased, but they're still emotionally loaded and don't yet know what they actually *want.* This movement does two jobs at once: it **settles the nervous system** enough to think clearly, and it **surfaces the person's real desires** so they can feed the next movement. It clears the fog *and* names the wants.

Backstage only. To the person this just feels like a warm turn toward what matters to them — never a "gratitude exercise."

## Summoned when

- **Emotional intensity** has come down from high but isn't calm; **outcome clarity** is still low (the person doesn't know what they want).
- **Skip it** when the person already knows what they want (high outcome clarity) — forcing it wastes their time and stalls the session. Go straight to formation.

## The handoff it owes the next movement

Whatever the person surfaces here — what they want, what they're grateful for, what they love — becomes raw material for the **Outcomes** step in formation. Orientation is not a detour; it's how the real outcome gets found instead of guessed.

## Lineage

A common priming / nervous-system-settling practice (sometimes "ten wants, ten gratitudes, ten loves"); no single claimed originator. Used here for settling + desire-surfacing, **not** as a manifestation gimmick. See `reference/attribution.md`.

## What it must NOT do

- Must not become a feel-good tangent. It's in service of clarity, not mood.
- Must not be run as a literal "list ten things" exercise on someone mid-problem — that's mechanical. Surface a few, gently, woven into the conversation.
- Must not be forced on a person who's already clear on what they want.
