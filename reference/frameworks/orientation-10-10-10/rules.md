# Framework rules — Orientation / Desires-gratitude-loves priming

## The three threads (woven, never listed)

Surface these gently and conversationally — a few real ones, not a forced list of ten:

1. **Desires — what do you actually want?** Not what you should want; what you'd choose if no one were watching. This is the load-bearing thread; it feeds the next movement's outcomes.
2. **Gratitude — what's genuinely good right now?** Even small. This is the nervous-system settle: it widens a narrowed, stressed view back out.
3. **Loves — what do you love, what lights you up?** Reconnects the person to themselves underneath the problem, so the decision gets made from a settled place rather than a panicked one.

## How to run it

- **Weave, don't quiz.** "Set the problem down for a second — if this were already handled, what would you actually want your week to feel like?" is orientation. "List ten things you're grateful for" is a worksheet. Do the former.
- **Two or three is plenty.** You're priming and surfacing, not journaling. A couple of real wants beats ten shallow ones.
- **Listen for the outcome hiding in the want.** When someone says "I just want to stop dreading Sunday," that's a desire *and* the seed of an outcome. Hold it for formation.
- **Let it settle them.** If the person visibly eases — longer sentences, less spiral — the settle worked; now they can think, and you can move to the real fork.

## Exit (by signal — never announced)

- When **outcome clarity** rises (the person can now name what they want) and intensity is workable → move toward **formation**, carrying the surfaced desires into the Outcomes step.
- If intensity spikes back up — the priming touched something raw — drop back to **fog** for a turn. That's not regression; it's the real thing surfacing.
